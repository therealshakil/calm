# Drift you accept — the calm move when a generative tool won't obey

**A lived-practice product** · 2026-07-06 · suite-wide (generative art)
*Companion to [`calm-generative-art.md`](./calm-generative-art.md) (the deterministic pole) and the
[drift spec](../../assets/drift/docs/specs/001-drift.md). Informs Reverie's reveal. Practice notes from one
piece, not sourced findings — hold loosely.*

## Question

calm's generative art so far is deterministic: [drift](../../assets/drift/docs/specs/001-drift.md) makes a
seed a written instruction — same seed, same art, full control. Reverie generates expressive art with a tool
you *can't* fully control (Midjourney): you write intent, and the machine returns something adjacent, or
something else. What is the calm move when the tool won't obey?

## Method

Lived practice, one piece. A poem was written first; a prompt was drafted from it; the tool was run; the
output diverged from the prompt (it returned a different subject entirely); the divergence was kept rather
than corrected; the result was finished as a quiet video loop. No literature pass — these are design
judgements from a single making, marked to firm up over more pieces.

## Findings

### 1. Two kinds of drift — control and accept
- **drift (the tool):** deterministic. You hold the seed; the art obeys. Drift-you-control.
- **Expressive AI generation:** you write a prompt for one subject and the machine hands back another. The
  calm move is not to fight for control — it is to *choose to keep* what it returned, or not.
  **Drift-you-accept.** Same word, opposite pole.

### 2. The gap between intent and output is the exhibit, not the error
- The prompt asked for one thing; the tool returned another; keeping it was the human act.
- "The skill shapes the prompt; the hands shape the art" — here the hands' work was **selection, not
  correction.** The person recommends and chooses; the machine never decides (the Article VII shape holds
  even here).

### 3. AI receipts are honest, not embarrassing
- The output carried visible artifacts — garbled signage, telltale render drift. The instinct to hide them
  is the wrong one for a gallery whose mandate is *the making is not hidden.*
- The reveal records the whole journey — poem → prompt → the accident → the choice to keep it — not just the
  polished end. The receipts are evidence of how it was made.

### 4. A calm video loop is a breath, not a blend
- To loop a short clip seamlessly, the reflex is a crossfade. But a crossfade double-exposes two different
  frames at the seam — it ghosts unless the frames are near-identical. Worse, placed at offset 0 the clip
  *opens* mid-blend: jarring on every repeat.
- A brief **fade to and from black** avoids the overlap entirely — nothing is ever double-exposed — and
  reads as a soft beat between repeats.
- This clears drift's own motion bar: *motion implied, opt-in, reduced-motion* — a breath fits; a churn
  would not (WCAG 2.2.2).

→ Confirmed in practice: fade-to-black beats crossfade for calm, near-still loops. A tail-only
micro-dissolve is the runner-up (starts clean, no black) when a dark beat is unwanted.

## Synthesis

- Expressive generation sits at the **opposite pole** from drift's determinism, but the same calm principles
  hold: the human selects (never the machine deciding), motion stays a breath, the making stays honest.
- The idea the suite did not yet have a name for: **drift-you-accept** — keeping what the tool gave over what
  you asked for is itself a calm, human act, not a failure of control. It belongs beside drift-you-control as
  the second half of the suite's relationship with generative art.
